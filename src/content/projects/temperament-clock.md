### The Question

What does it mean, physically and musically, for twelve pure fifths to fail to return to the octave?

The temperament clock began as a way to make the Pythagorean comma less abstract. The premise is simple: let the listener move through fifths, hear the accumulating discrepancy, and see the cycle resist closure.

### The Structure I Found

The circle of fifths looks visually complete, but the frequency relationships underneath it are not symmetrical in the same way. Stacking perfect 3:2 fifths twelve times overshoots seven octaves by a small but audible amount.

That gap is where temperament enters. The clock turns the theory into a navigable object: a cycle, an offset, and a listening surface.

### The Artefact

You can open the clock at [temperament-clock.vercel.app](https://temperament-clock.vercel.app).

It is meant to be used more than read. Step through the fifths, listen for the drift, and watch the circle reveal the small error that temperament has to place somewhere.

### What It Shows

It shows that tuning is not just a technical compromise. It is a choice about which relationships get to remain pure, which ones absorb the error, and how a musical system distributes tension.

### What I Learned

Interactive explanation works best when it lets the concept push back. The useful moment is not when the interface explains the comma, but when the listener expects closure and hears that closure fail.
